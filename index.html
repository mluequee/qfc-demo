A real experience

A missed call that almost cost a local business a job they never knew they lost.

My AC unit was having issues. I did what most people do — pulled up Google and looked for the best-rated HVAC company in my area. Found one with strong reviews and called them.

No answer. The voicemail told me his mailbox was full and gave me a different number to call or text. I wasn't ready for that. I didn't have anything to write it down with. So I hung up.

My first thought was to just call someone else. So I took a minute, found something to write with, and called back.

He answered the second time. Turned out he was finishing up with another customer which is exactly why he hadn't picked up. We set an appointment, he came out, fixed the problem quickly and charged a fair price. I understood immediately why his reviews were so strong.

But I kept thinking about it. If I hadn't called back, that was a job he never knew he lost, to a customer who never complained, who just quietly called someone else. Think about what that job would have been worth to you. Then think about how many times a week that might be happening.

Why this matters

This isn't just your business. It's how people search now.

When someone needs a plumber, an HVAC tech, an electrician — they pull up Google, find a few names, and start calling down the list. If you don't pick up, most of them don't leave a voicemail and most of them don't call back. They just dial the next name. You never see that call, so you never know it happened.

That's the part that's hard to feel: the calls you know you missed are bad enough. The ones that hurt are the ones you never see — someone who called once, got nothing, and quietly went with someone else.

The fix isn't answering every call. You can't. You're on a job. The fix is making sure that when you can't pick up, the person hears from you fast enough that they don't go looking somewhere else before you're free to call them back.

How it works

Three things happen automatically. You do none of them.

1
Someone calls your number and you miss it. You're on a job, under a crawlspace, with another customer. Doesn't matter why.
2
They hear a short message that a text is on the way, and seconds later it arrives: "Hi, this is Quality Flow Control. We missed your call but we got your number and will be in touch shortly." They stop looking for someone else.
3
You get a text with their number and the time they called. If they reply to the text, their reply lands on your phone too. You call back when you're free. The job is still yours.
A fast response is what keeps people from wandering. When someone calls and hears nothing, they keep dialing. When they get a text back right away, they have a reason to wait for you instead of going to the next name on the list.
